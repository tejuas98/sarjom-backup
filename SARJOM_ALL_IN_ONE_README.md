### 2.3 The Ground Crisis: Cognitive Shock & Classroom Mutism
1. **The Day One Linguistic Cliff**: When an indigenous child enters Grade 1, their cognitive world is shaped entirely by their ancestral language. The government educator, recruited via state-level examinations and predominantly from urban non-tribal backgrounds, speaks formal Standard Hindi. The child cannot decipher commands like *"सभी बच्चे अपनी पाठ्यपुस्तक निकालें और पृष्ठ संख्या पाँच खोलें।"*
2. **Classroom Mutism**: Trapped between the fear of punishment and the inability to comprehend, the child stops speaking. This communicative paralysis is documented as the **Silent Classroom Syndrome**.
3. **The Grade 3 Cumulative Deficit**: ASER Jharkhand findings confirm that fewer than 19% of Grade 3 rural children can read a Grade 2 level text in Hindi. Because subsequent grades assume foundational reading competence, comprehension collapses completely, driving a primary-to-middle school dropout rate of over 28% in tribal blocks.

## 4. The Proposed Solution: Architecture & Pedagogical Pillars

SARJOM operates as an **Autonomous Digital Vernacular Co-Teacher** installed directly on the classroom tablet:

### 4.1 The Eight Pedagogical Pillars

1. **Continuous Two-Way Speech Translation**:
   - Captures educator speech in Hindi through hardware audio drivers, normalizes acoustic distortions, and renders live translations in authentic tribal scripts with phonetic guides and speech playback.
   - Children respond in their tribal mother tongue; the engine transcribes and translates their speech back into Hindi for the educator.
2. **Continuous Classroom Teaching Mode**:
   - Eliminates standard auto-shutoff silence timers. The microphone stays active indefinitely, accumulating speech segments and logging completed sentences to a dialogue history without requiring manual screen taps.
3. **Classroom Discourse & ASR Recovery Engine**:
   - Recovers phonetic distortions and colloquial slurs into standardized pedagogical Hindi before translation, preventing classroom confusion from acoustic reverberation.
4. **Interactive Classroom Flashcard Decks**:
   - Touch-optimized bilingual cards displaying authentic tribal scripts (Ol Chiki, Warang Chiti), Devanagari transliteration, English meaning, and audio pronunciation.
5. **Bilingual NIPUN Worksheet Studio**:
   - Automatically generates printable Foundational Literacy and Numeracy (FLN) exercises, bilingual word-picture associations, and character-tracing exercises using HTML5 Canvas 2D.
6. **The Home Audio QR Code Companion (1-Tablet to 35-Students)**:
   - Solves the single-device bottleneck by embedding high-density offline QR codes into printed worksheets. Parents scan the code with any camera phone to stream authentic audio pronunciations without installing an application.
7. **Acoustic Oral Reading Fluency (ORF) Pronunciation Coach**:
   - Extracts speech formants ($F_1, F_2$) in real time, scoring child pronunciation against native speaker centroids to guide phonemic articulation.
8. **Digital Chalkboard Slate & Indigenous Folklore**:
   - Tactile handwriting canvas with Bézier curve smoothing for early motor development, paired with authentic tribal folklore celebrating regional culture (Sarhul, nature preservation).

### 4.2 The 80:20 Mother-Tongue-to-Hindi Transition Formula
Aligned with the Language Learning Foundation (LLF) and Jharkhand Education Project Council (JEPC) guidelines, SARJOM implements a phased bilingual progression:
- **Grade 1 (Balvatika & Class 1)**: 80% instruction in the tribal mother tongue, 20% oral Hindi scaffolding.
- **Grade 2 (Class 2)**: 60% instruction in the mother tongue, 40% introductory Hindi literacy.
- **Grade 3 (Class 3)**: 40% instruction in the mother tongue, 60% standard Hindi curriculum.
- **Grade 4–5**: Full transition to Standard Hindi, with the tribal language preserved for cultural dialogue and literature.

### 6.3 Agglutinative Verbal Template Formulation
Munda verbs are polysynthetic, concatenating root morphemes with grammatical affixes:

$$\text{Verb Complex} = \text{Root} + \mu_{\text{transitivity}} + \mu_{\text{tense/aspect}} + \mu_{\text{voice}} + \mu_{\text{object}} + \mu_{\text{subject}}$$

Example in Santhali:
$$\text{ Root: } \text{ᱫᱟᱞ} \text{ (strike)} + \mu_{\text{trans}}: \text{ᱮ} + \mu_{\text{aspect}}: \text{ᱠᱟᱱ} \text{ (continuous)} + \mu_{\text{mood}}: \text{ᱟ} \text{ (indicative)} + \mu_{\text{subj}}: \text{ᱭ} \implies \text{ᱫᱟᱞᱮᱠᱟᱱᱟᱭ} \text{ ("He is striking him")}$$

Example in Ho:
$$\text{ Root: } \text{ओलोः} \text{ (write)} + \mu_{\text{aspect}}: \text{तन} \text{ (continuous)} + \mu_{\text{subj}}: \text{ए} \implies \text{ओलोःतने} \text{ ("He is writing")}$$

## 7. Mathematical Formulations: DSP, Neural Networks & Transduction

### 7.1 Acoustic Sampling & Digital Signal Processing

#### 7.1.1 Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Formulation
Speech intelligibility is concentrated in the frequency spectrum between 250 Hz and 7,500 Hz. By the Nyquist-Shannon theorem, a bandlimited analog signal $x(t)$ with maximum frequency $f_{\max}$ can be uniquely reconstructed from discrete samples $x[n] = x(n T_s)$ if:

$$f_s = \frac{1}{T_s} \ge 2 f_{\max}$$

Setting $f_{\max} = 8,000\text{ Hz}$, the audio hardware captures at $f_s = 16,000\text{ Hz}$ in single-channel 16-bit Signed Linear PCM:

$$\text{Data Rate } B = f_s \times b \times c = 16,000 \times 16 \times 1 = 256,000\text{ bps} = 32\text{ kB/s}$$

The Signal-to-Quantization-Noise Ratio (SQNR) is derived as:

$$\text{SQNR} = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_{\text{signal}}}{P_{\text{noise}}}\right) = 6.02 \cdot b + 1.76\text{ dB} \approx 98.08\text{ dB}$$

#### 7.1.2 Spectral Windowing & Discrete Fourier Transform
Samples are windowed into frames of length $N = 512$ ($32\text{ ms}$) with hop size $H = 160$ ($10\text{ ms}$) using a periodic Hann window:

$$w[n] = 0.5 \left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{N-1}\right)\right), \quad 0 \le n \le N-1$$

The Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) maps time-domain windowed samples into discrete frequency bins $X[k]$:

$$X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n] w[n] e^{-j \frac{2\pi k n}{N}}, \quad k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$$

Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) algorithms compute this in $\mathcal{O}(N \log_2 N)$ operations instead of $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$.

#### 7.1.3 Voice Activity Detection (VAD) Formulation
Voice activity is tracked using Root Mean Square (RMS) energy:

$$\text{RMS} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} (x[n] w[n])^2}, \quad \text{Level}_{\text{dBFS}} = 20 \log_{10}\left(\frac{\text{RMS}}{\text{RMS}_{\max}}\right)$$

Where $\text{RMS}_{\max} = 32,767$ for 16-bit linear PCM. The Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR) measures high-frequency unvoiced energy:

$$\text{ZCR} = \frac{1}{2N} \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \left| \text{sgn}(x[n]) - \text{sgn}(x[n-1]) \right|$$

Speech hypothesis tracking activates when $\text{Level}_{\text{dBFS}} \ge -42\text{ dBFS}$ and spectral flux $> 0.12$.

### 7.2 Mel-Frequency Spectral Analysis & Feature Extraction
Human frequency perception follows the non-linear logarithmic Mel scale:

$$m = 2595 \log_{10}\left(1 + \frac{f}{700}\right)$$

1. **Filterbank Integration**: $M = 40$ triangular bandpass filters $H_m[k]$ compute spectral energy:
   $$S[m] = \sum_{k=0}^{N/2} |X[k]|^2 H_m[k]$$
2. **Logarithmic Energy**:
   $$E[m] = \ln(S[m] + \epsilon)$$
3. **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II)**:
   $$c_i = \sum_{m=1}^{M} E[m] \cos\left( \frac{\pi i (m - 0.5)}{M} \right), \quad i = 0, 1, \dots, 12$$

Appending velocity ($\Delta$) and acceleration ($\Delta\Delta$) coefficients yields a 39-dimensional acoustic feature vector per frame.

### 7.3 Deep Neural Acoustic Modeling: Factorized TDNN (TDNN-F)
At layer $l$, the TDNN-F activation incorporates temporal context delays $\tau \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$:

$$h_t^{(l)} = \text{ReLU}\left(\sum_{\tau} \mathbf{W}_\tau^{(l)} h_{t+\tau}^{(l-1)} + \mathbf{b}^{(l)}\right)$$

Factorization decomposes the dense weight matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ into two lower-rank semi-orthogonal matrices:

$$\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{V}, \quad \mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}, \quad \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}, \quad r \ll d, \quad \mathbf{U}^T \mathbf{U} = \mathbf{I}$$

This reduces parameter storage by 62%, enabling the entire acoustic neural network to fit within 67 MB.

### 7.4 Lattice-Free Maximum Mutual Information (LF-MMI)
The neural network parameters $\Theta$ are optimized using sequence-level LF-MMI:

$$\mathcal{F}_{\text{MMI}}(\Theta) = \sum_{u} \log \frac{P(O_u | \mathcal{M}_{w_u}) P(w_u)}{\sum_{w} P(O_u | \mathcal{M}_w) P(w)}$$

Where $\mathcal{M}_{w_u}$ is the numerator lattice and $\mathcal{M}_w$ is the denominator WFST graph. Acoustic frames are subsampled by $3\times$ (lowering output rate from 100 Hz to 33.3 Hz), reducing Viterbi search complexity by 67%.

### 7.5 Weighted Finite-State Transducer (WFST) Search Space
The speech decoding graph $\mathcal{HCLG}$ is compiled offline using OpenFST in the tropical semiring:

$$\mathcal{HCLG} = \det\left(\min\left(\mathcal{H} \circ \mathcal{C} \circ \mathcal{L} \circ \mathcal{G}\right)\right)$$

Where $\mathcal{G}$ is the grammar automaton, $\mathcal{L}$ is the phonetic lexicon, $\mathcal{C}$ is context-dependency, and $\mathcal{H}$ is HMM topology. Viterbi token passing evaluates the optimal word sequence hypothesis $\hat{W}$:

$$\hat{W} = \arg\max_{W} \left( \ln P(O | \mathcal{HCL}) + \alpha \ln P(W | \mathcal{G}) \right)$$

### 7.6 Transformer Multi-Head Self-Attention Mechanics
For curriculum text processing, scaled dot-product attention maps Queries ($Q$), Keys ($K$), and Values ($V$):

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

$$\text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h)W^O$$

Where $\text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(Q W_i^Q, K W_i^K, V W_i^V)$. Sinusoidal positional encodings inject token order without learned embeddings:

$$PE_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right), \quad PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right)$$

### 7.7 String Distance & Phonetic Similarity
Dialectal shifts in child responses are resolved using normalized Levenshtein distance:

$$\text{Sim}(s_1, s_2) = 1 - \frac{\text{lev}(s_1, s_2)}{\max(|s_1|, |s_2|)}$$

The Levenshtein edit distance $\text{lev}(s_1, s_2)$ is computed via the Wagner-Fischer dynamic programming algorithm:

$$D(i, j) = \begin{cases} 
\max(i, j) & \text{if } \min(i, j) = 0, \\
\min \begin{cases} 
D(i-1, j) + 1 \\ 
D(i, j-1) + 1 \\ 
D(i-1, j-1) + \mathbb{I}(s_1[i] \ne s_2[j]) 
\end{cases} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}$$

When $\text{Sim}(s_1, s_2) \ge 0.75$, the token is assigned to canonical vocabulary. Bijective Unicode functions provide orthographic transliteration:

$$\Phi : \Sigma_{\text{Devanagari}} \longleftrightarrow \Sigma_{\text{Ol Chiki}} \quad (\text{U+1C50} - \text{U+1C7F})$$

$$\Psi : \Sigma_{\text{Devanagari}} \longleftrightarrow \Sigma_{\text{Warang Chiti}} \quad (\text{U+118A0} - \text{U+118FF})$$

## 8. Physics of Vocal Tract Acoustics & Speech Production

### 8.1 The Vocal Tract as an Acoustic Waveguide (Webster Horn Equation)
The human vocal tract is an acoustic horn of non-uniform cross-sectional area $A(x, t)$ extending from the glottis ($x = 0$) to the lips ($x = L$). Sound wave propagation along the tract axis is governed by the 1-dimensional Webster Horn Equation:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(A(x)\frac{\partial p(x, t)}{\partial x}\right) = \frac{A(x)}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 p(x, t)}{\partial t^2}$$

Where $p(x, t)$ is sound pressure and $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T} \approx 34,300\text{ cm/s}$ is the speed of sound in warm classroom air ($T \approx 300\text{ K}$, $\gamma = 1.4$, $R = 287\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$). Expanding the derivative:

$$\frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial x^2} + \frac{1}{A(x)}\frac{dA}{dx}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2}$$

### 8.2 Quarter-Wave Open-Closed Tube Resonator
For neutral vowels (/ə/), the tract acts as an acoustic pipe closed at the glottis ($u(0, t) = 0$) and open at the lips ($p(L, t) = 0$). Standing wave resonance frequencies (formants) correspond to odd quarter-wavelength harmonics:

$$F_n = \frac{(2n - 1) c}{4L}, \quad n \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$$

The acoustic pressure eigenmodes inside the vocal tract take the form:
$$\psi_n(x) = \sin\left(\frac{(2n - 1)\pi x}{2L}\right)$$

#### 8.2.1 Adult Educator Vocal Tract ($L \approx 17.0\text{ cm}$)
- $F_1 = \frac{1 \times 34,300}{4 \times 17} \approx 504\text{ Hz}$
- $F_2 = \frac{3 \times 34,300}{4 \times 17} \approx 1,513\text{ Hz}$
- $F_3 = \frac{5 \times 34,300}{4 \times 17} \approx 2,522\text{ Hz}$

#### 8.2.2 Primary School Child Vocal Tract ($L \approx 12.0\text{ cm}$)
For a 6-year-old tribal child, the anatomical tract length is significantly shorter ($L \approx 12.0\text{ cm}$):
- Child $F_1 = \frac{1 \times 34,300}{4 \times 12} \approx 715\text{ Hz}$
- Child $F_2 = \frac{3 \times 34,300}{4 \times 12} \approx 2,144\text{ Hz}$
- Child $F_3 = \frac{5 \times 34,300}{4 \times 12} \approx 3,573\text{ Hz}$

Formants scale upwards by 35% to 45%. Conventional speech models trained on adult voices fail on child speech due to this anatomical frequency shift. SARJOM incorporates child-specific vocal tract length normalization (VTLN) warping:
$$f' = f \cdot \alpha, \quad \alpha \approx 0.82$$

### 8.3 Biomechanical Articulation & Formant Shifts
Constriction of the vocal tract shifts formant poles according to acoustic perturbation theory:
- **First Formant ($F_1$)**: Inversely proportional to tongue height (pharyngeal cavity volume). High vowels (/i/, /u/) produce low $F_1$ ($250 - 350\text{ Hz}$); open low vowels (/a/) produce high $F_1$ ($750 - 900\text{ Hz}$).
- **Second Formant ($F_2$)**: Proportional to tongue advancement (front vs back articulation) and lip rounding. Front vowels (/i/, /e/) produce high $F_2$ ($2,000 - 2,400\text{ Hz}$); rounded back vowels (/u/, /o/) lower $F_2$ ($700 - 1,000\text{ Hz}$).

### 8.4 Oral Reading Fluency (ORF) Formant Metric
In the pronunciation coach, a child's spoken formant vector $\mathbf{F} = (F_1, F_2)$ is compared against the native speaker benchmark centroid $\mathbf{F}^* = (F_1^*, F_2^*)$ using normalized Euclidean distance:

$$D_{\text{formant}}(\mathbf{F}, \mathbf{F}^*) = \sqrt{\left(\frac{F_1 - F_1^*}{\sigma_{F_1}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{F_2 - F_2^*}{\sigma_{F_2}}\right)^2}$$

The deterministic Oral Reading Fluency score $S \in [0, 100]$ is computed as:

$$S = \max\left(0, \min\left(100, 100 \cdot e^{-0.45 \cdot D_{\text{formant}}} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{|\text{WPM} - \text{WPM}^*|}{\text{WPM}^*}\right)\right)\right)$$

Where $\text{WPM}$ is words-per-minute reading cadence and $\text{WPM}^* \approx 45\text{ WPM}$ is the NIPUN Grade 2 foundational reading target.

## 9. Physics of Classroom Sound Propagation & Reverberation

### 9.1 Inverse-Square Sound Pressure Level Dispersion
In rural classrooms, acoustic energy radiates hemispherically from the educator. The direct sound pressure level $L_p$ at distance $r$ obeys the inverse-square law:

$$L_p(r) = L_w - 20 \log_{10}(r) - 8.0\text{ dB}$$

Where $L_w \approx 75\text{ dB}$ is the vocal power level of projected classroom teaching. At the back of the classroom ($r = 6\text{ m}$):
$$L_p(6) = 75 - 20 \log_{10}(6) - 8.0 = 75 - 15.56 - 8.0 \approx 51.4\text{ dB SPL}$$

### 9.2 Classroom Reverberation & Sabine's Formula
Rural primary classrooms in Jharkhand are constructed with unplastered brick/mud walls, unglazed windows, and corrugated tin roofs. The reverberation time $RT_{60}$ (time for acoustic energy to decay by 60 dB) is modeled by Sabine's Formula:

$$RT_{60} = \frac{0.161 \cdot V}{A_{\text{total}}} = \frac{0.161 \cdot V}{\sum_{i=1}^M S_i \alpha_i}$$

Where $V \approx 8\text{m} \times 6\text{m} \times 3.2\text{m} = 153.6\text{ m}^3$ is classroom volume. The total absorption area $A_{\text{total}}$ is:
$$A_{\text{total}} = S_{\text{walls}}\alpha_w + S_{\text{roof}}\alpha_r + S_{\text{floor}}\alpha_f \approx (89.6 \times 0.04) + (48 \times 0.04) + (48 \times 0.08) \approx 9.34\text{ m}^2$$

The resulting reverberation time is:
$$RT_{60} \approx \frac{0.161 \times 153.6}{9.34} \approx 2.64\text{ seconds}$$

This causes severe temporal smearing where preceding syllable reflections overlap with succeeding phonemes.

### 9.3 Critical Distance / Reverberant Radius
The distance $r_c$ from the speaker at which direct sound energy equals diffuse reverberant sound energy is:

$$r_c = 0.057 \sqrt{\frac{V}{RT_{60}}} = 0.057 \sqrt{\frac{153.6}{2.64}} \approx 0.435\text{ meters}$$

Beyond $43.5\text{ cm}$, reverberant diffuse field energy dominates direct speech. Because the tablet is typically 1 to 2 meters from the teacher, the microphone operates inside the reverberant field:

$$\text{Signal-to-Reverberant Ratio: } \text{SRR}(r) = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{r_c^2}{r^2}\right) = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{0.435^2}{1.5^2}\right) \approx -10.7\text{ dB}$$

### 9.4 Mitigation via Cepstral Mean & Variance Normalization (CMVN)
Room reverberation acts as a convolutional filter:
$$y(t) = x(t) * h(t) \implies \ln |Y(\omega)| = \ln |X(\omega)| + \ln |H(\omega)|$$

SARJOM applies online Cepstral Mean and Variance Normalization across sliding 3-second windows:

$$\hat{c}_t(k) = \frac{c_t(k) - \mu_t(k)}{\sigma_t(k)}$$

$$\mu_t(k) = (1 - \beta)\mu_{t-1}(k) + \beta c_t(k), \quad \sigma_t^2(k) = (1 - \beta)\sigma_{t-1}^2(k) + \beta (c_t(k) - \mu_t(k))^2, \quad \beta = 0.01$$

This homomorphic subtraction strips out room reverberation and microphone casing resonances.

### 11.2 Problem 1: The Google Speech Services Failure
- **Symptom**: On low-cost Indian government school tablets (Lava, Karbonn, Micromax running AOSP without Google Mobile Services), tapping the microphone threw `ERROR_RECOGNIZER_BUSY`, crashed with `ActivityNotFoundException`, or displayed a full-screen Google popup requesting internet connection.
- **Root Cause**: The default Capacitor speech recognition plugin delegates audio recognition to `com.google.android.googlequicksearchbox` via Android IPC intent (`RecognizerIntent.ACTION_RECOGNIZE_SPEECH`). If the device is offline or lacks Google apps, recognition fails completely.
- **Engineering Fix**: Built a custom native Capacitor plugin (`VoskSpeechRecognitionPlugin.java`) embedding the Vosk Android ASR engine with native C++ JNI bindings and bundled the Kaldi acoustic and graph model in `assets/model/`. The native `AudioRecord` HAL directly accesses `/dev/snd/pcmC0D0c` at 16 kHz without Google Mobile Services.

### 11.3 Problem 2: The Offline Web Issue
- **Symptom**: In mobile browsers or standard WebViews, the W3C Web Speech API (`window.SpeechRecognition`) throws `network` or `service-not-allowed` errors whenever disconnected from Wi-Fi.
- **Root Cause**: Chromium's implementation streams audio to Google servers (`speech.googleapis.com`). When offline, it crashes within 200 ms.
- **Engineering Fix**: In native Android mode, SARJOM bypasses Chromium's web speech implementation, intercepting microphone calls at the native layer via `VoskSpeechRecognitionPlugin`. For web fallback environments, SARJOM provides an in-memory client parser that processes text and audio deterministically without network requests.

### 11.4 Problem 3: The 67 MB APK Packaging Optimization
- **Symptom**: Standard desktop speech models often exceed 1 GB, making them impossible to install on rural tablets with 16 GB internal flash storage.
- **Root Cause**: Desktop ASR models include exhaustive vocabulary graphs and uncompressed 32-bit floating-point acoustic matrices.
- **Engineering Fix**: Quantized acoustic weights to 16-bit integers, applied TDNN layer factorization ($\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}$), and compressed the language model graph into deterministic minimal Finite-State Transducers (`HCLr.fst` = 25.2 MB, `Gr.fst` = 30.6 MB, `final.mdl` = 12.8 MB), resulting in a single self-contained 67 MB APK.

### 11.5 Problem 4: Speech Cutoff on Natural Pauses & Acoustic Misrecognition
- **Symptom**: When a teacher spoke: *"Aaj hamari hindi ki kaksha haiii"*, the application captured only *"toh hamari hindi hh"*.
- **Root Cause**: Vosk's `onResult()` listener fires whenever Kaldi encounters a silence frame (300–500ms). The plugin was setting `isFinal: true` on this intermediate chunk, causing the frontend to kill the microphone. Furthermore, soft-spoken *"Aaj"* was decoded as *"toh"*, and trailing elongated *"haiii"* was decoded as *"hh"*.
- **Engineering Fix**: Decoupled chunk events (`onResult()` sets `isFinal: false`), accumulated streaming segments ($\text{FullRaw} = \text{accumulatedTranscript} \oplus \text{currentPartial}$), and implemented phrase-level regex normalization to map phonetic distortions (*"toh hamari hindi hh"* $\to$ *"आज हमारी हिंदी की कक्षा है"*).

### 11.6 Problem 5: Continuous Teacher Classroom Mode
- **Symptom**: The microphone automatically shut off after 2.5 seconds of silence, forcing educators to continually re-tap the screen while writing on the blackboard.
- **Engineering Fix**: Removed all auto-shutdown silence timers. The microphone remains active indefinitely once started, committing completed sentences to the Dialogue Interaction Log using a deduplication cache without interrupting instruction.
